01

Strategy & operations · Primary research

Morrisons Market Street: Strategy & Operations Review

A client-standard review of why Morrisons closed its service counters, then started reopening them — built partly from working behind them.

What

A 19-slide strategy and operations review of Morrisons closing 70 Market Street counters in 2025 as "uneconomic", then reopening them in 2026 as "part of our DNA". Built from first-hand counter experience and 104 tiered public sources.

Why

The two decisions contradicted each other, and I work on Market Street — so instead of taking either at face value I went and checked what was underneath both. It's exactly the kind of open-ended commercial question I like getting stuck into.

How

Benchmarked pricing against Greggs, Asda and Subway, mapped the counter economics, and built four recommendations — labelling every claim by evidence type, using no invented figures, and dropping five of my own ideas when the evidence didn't back them.

19 slides · 104 sources · 4 recommendations. The most original piece here — primary research on a live decision, written to the standard of a consulting deck.

02

SQL · Power BI · Data modelling

E-Commerce Commercial Intelligence

Turning 1M+ real UK online-retail transactions into a management dashboard.

What

An end-to-end analytics build on the UCI Online Retail II dataset — over a million transactions from a UK online retailer. I cleaned and modelled the raw data in SQL, then built a four-page Power BI dashboard covering sales, customers, products, geography and cancellations.

Why

To show I can take a large, messy, real dataset — not a tutorial file — and turn it into something a manager could actually decide from. The goal wasn't more charts, it was findings someone would act on.

How

Loaded the data into PostgreSQL and wrote cleaning rules for returns, missing customer IDs and bad prices. Built a star schema and analytical views with CTEs, joins and window functions, then layered ~15 DAX measures and an RFM-style customer segmentation on top.

What it surfaced: the UK drives 85% of revenue, sales peak sharply every November, roughly 72% of customers come back to buy again, and cancellations run at about 15% of orders — each with a clear "so what" for the business.

03

Python · LLM pipeline · Streamlit

AI Investment Memo Assistant

An annual report goes in; a cited investment memo comes out — every claim traceable to the source.

What

A pipeline that reads a company's annual report and produces a structured investment memo where every material claim links back to a verified quote from the original PDF. A small web app lets you click any reference and see the exact source text behind it.

Why

Language models make things up. I wanted to find out whether you could get genuinely useful, analyst-style output from one while keeping it honest — every number and statement checkable against the document it came from.

How

The system extracts facts, verifies each against the source text, stores them in a register, then writes the memo from that register with citations. The principle: the AI reads and writes the prose, but code does the counting and checking.

Tested, not just built: scored against a 50-item gold set on Auto Trader — 90% fact recall, 80% risk coverage — then run again on Greggs and Games Workshop to check it generalised beyond the company it was tuned on.

04

Equity research · Valuation · Financial modelling

Integrated 3-Statement Models: Tesco & Diageo

Two full valuation models built from scratch, every number a live formula.

What

Two integrated three-statement models on Tesco and Diageo, each with a DCF, trading comps and a Base/Bull/Bear scenario toggle, plus a written equity research note with a rating and target price.

Why

To prove I can build a valuation the way equity research and IB analysts do — traceable to the filings, driven by assumptions I can defend, not a black box that spits out a number.

How

Built segmental revenue, IFRS 16 leases, PP&E and debt schedules by hand, drove valuation off WACC and terminal growth with sensitivity tables, and wrote Python scripts to validate every historical input against the published accounts.

BUY — Tesco 310p (+19%), Diageo 2,800p (+12%). Around 933 live formulas per model, zero errors, every historical input machine-checked against the filings.

05

Private equity · LBO modelling

Morrisons LBO: CD&R Take-Private (2021)

A formula-driven model of a real £9.9bn private-equity deal, and where the returns come from.

What

A fully formula-driven LBO model and transaction case study of CD&R's £9.9bn take-private of Wm Morrison — sources and uses, a four-tranche debt schedule with cash sweep, returns, and two-way sensitivity tables.

Why

To understand how a real private-equity deal is actually put together and where the returns come from — and to be able to defend the assumptions rather than just report an IRR.

How

Built the operating model and debt waterfall from the deal's public terms, ran a returns bridge to separate EBITDA growth from multiple and leverage effects, and stress-tested the case with sensitivity tables.

Base case: 8.9% IRR · 1.53x MoM. 422 live formulas, zero errors, with a value bridge that shows exactly which lever drives the return.

easyJet FY2025: Audit Risk Review

An audit-planning memo on a listed airline, built from its real annual report.

What

An audit-style risk review of easyJet plc's FY2025 accounts, focused on where a real audit team would spend its time: revenue and deferred income, lease and maintenance provisions, asset carrying values and liquidity.

Why
